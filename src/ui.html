A TRANSPARENT PROCESS

Follow the evidence.

Every number needs a source. Missing data stays missing.

1. Match the journal

Resolve publisher homepages by ISSN with OpenAlex. Search titles or topics with the public Crossref API. Topic searches match journal metadata; they are discovery aids, not manuscript suitability scores. Match the ISSN before attaching a journal or issue URL. Publisher-page collection supports ScienceDirect, Springer Nature, Nature, MDPI, Frontiers, Wiley, Oxford Academic, PLOS, BioMed Central, Taylor & Francis and SAGE. Other publishers can be audited using manual histories.

2. Require SCIE. Exclude ESCI.

The Clarivate Master Journal List is the authoritative indexing check. Its detailed access may require registration or a subscription. A publisher’s indexing statement is labeled publisher evidence. Extract the Electronic ISSN and Print ISSN from Journal information and search either identifier in MJL. Record the matching coverage statement and source. If direct MJL access is unavailable, a matched publisher or institutional journal record can confirm SCIE separately, with its authority and source shown. Generic Web of Science mentions and search snippets cannot pass. An Impact Factor, Scopus listing, or the words “Web of Science” do not establish SCIE. ESCI evidence always triggers exclusion, including conflicting SCIE and ESCI claims. Unknown indexing never passes the SCIE filter. Attach a dated Clarivate check manually when automated access is unavailable.

3. Keep citation metrics separate

Impact Factor is a Clarivate metric; CiteScore is a Scopus metric. We extract explicit labels on the journal page and retain the quoted evidence, source, year and check date. If a year is absent it is shown as unspecified, never inferred. Check Scopus Sources or Journal Citation Reports for missing or conflicting metrics. Your example values, 17.3 and 10.3, are not hardcoded as current metrics.

4. Audit at least 15 articles

Choose the latest issue or a recent regular issue. Load its article links, then inspect article history pages in listed order. Add more recent issues if needed. Crossref supplies an optional fallback list of recent publications; these are clearly labeled and are not proof of latest-issue membership. The tool never substitutes publication, online, deposit, or first-decision dates for acceptance. Missing Revised dates remain missing. With multiple reported revisions, the last revision is used.

An article counts toward a complete audit when Received and Accepted are present, ordered, not in the future, and issue membership is supplied. Revised dates are retained when reported and flagged as not reported otherwise; an invalid supplied revision date still excludes the row. The tool stops after 15 verified acceptance intervals; automatic research can inspect up to 240 candidates across 48 issues per pass and resume unresolved checks. You may add more. All valid Received–Accepted pairs contribute to preliminary statistics, even when a revision date is missing. The median, mean, range and interquartile range use calendar days. Invalid date sequences and obvious editorials, corrections, retractions and errata are excluded. Check article types and special-issue membership in the sources.

Published and accepted articles are a selected sample. These intervals describe the sampled accepted papers, not the probability of acceptance or a guarantee of turnaround for a new submission. Missing histories can bias the sample. First decision often includes desk rejections and is not an acceptance estimate.

5. Keep the audit trail

Export the report as JSON or article rows as CSV. Manual entries retain their provenance. No external account credentials are needed or stored. Saved reports live in browser storage; live API calls go to Crossref and supported publishers. Publisher access restrictions are respected; this tool does not bypass paywalls or bot checks.
